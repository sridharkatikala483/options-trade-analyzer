# Implied volatility

## What it measures
Implied volatility (IV) is the market's forecast of how much a stock will move, backed out from option prices. It is quoted as an annualized percentage: an IV of 30% means the market prices roughly a one-standard-deviation move of 30% over a year. Higher IV means more expensive options, because larger expected moves make the option more likely to finish in the money.

## Judging whether IV is high or low
IV only means something relative to the stock's own history. A 40% IV is ordinary for a volatile growth stock but unusually high for a large, steady company. Compare the current IV with the stock's typical range over the past year, and with the IV of other expirations. Buying options when IV is unusually high means paying a premium that can shrink even if the stock moves the way you expected.
