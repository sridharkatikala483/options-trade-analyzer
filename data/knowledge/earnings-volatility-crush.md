# Volatility crush after earnings

## Why IV rises before earnings
Before a scheduled earnings report, uncertainty about the result pushes implied volatility up, especially in the expiration that falls just after the announcement. Option buyers pay this extra premium for the chance of a large move.

## What happens after the announcement
Once results are public, the uncertainty disappears and implied volatility usually drops sharply, often within minutes of the next market open. This is called volatility crush. The option loses value from the IV drop even if the stock moves in the buyer's favor.

## Being right and still losing money
A trader can correctly predict the direction of the earnings move and still lose money on a long option, if the move is smaller than the one priced in. To profit, the stock generally must move more than the market's expected move, not merely in the right direction. Repricing the option at a lower post-earnings volatility shows how large a move is really needed to break even.
