# Choosing an expiration

## Matching time to the thesis
The expiration should give the expected move enough time to happen. If a trader expects a stock to rise over the next month, a contract expiring this week forces the move into a few days. A later expiration costs more up front but loses less value per day.

## Expirations around events
If an expiration falls just after earnings or another scheduled event, its price includes extra event volatility. Choosing an expiration before the event avoids paying for it; choosing one well after reduces the share of the premium that depends on the event.
